Why Simple, Fast Web Pages Feel Better to Read

Notes from learning how line length, whitespace, and a good font scale make long articles way easier on the eyes.

By Alex M. • March 28, 2020 • 6 min read

In this article

  2. Line length matters
  3. Fluid sizing with clamp()
  4. Pictures need context
  5. Wrapping up

Everyone has clicked a link to read a quick recipe or tutorial, only to get hit by three popups, an auto-playing video in the corner, and text squished into a tiny strip in the middle. When I started learning HTML and CSS this year, I realized how rare a calm, readable page actually is.

Good editorial design is mostly about getting out of the reader's way. You do not need ten JavaScript libraries to make an article feel like a real magazine. Semantic HTML tags like <article>, <figure>, and <blockquote> already give the page a solid skeleton before you write a single line of CSS.

Line length matters more than I thought

One of the first mistakes I made was letting paragraphs stretch across the entire 1440px screen. When a line is 160 characters wide, your eyes lose their place every time you jump down to the next line.

“Aiming for roughly 60 to 75 characters per line makes even plain black text on white paper feel intentional.”

Using max-width: 65ch on the article column is a neat trick because the ch unit is based on the width of the zero character in your chosen font. So if you bump up the font size, the column scales with it automatically.

Fluid sizing with clamp()

Before learning about clamp(), I had to write three different @media queries just to make headings look right on phones, tablets, and laptops. With clamp(min, preferred, max), the browser smoothly scales the text between a minimum and maximum size based on viewport width.

Pictures and diagrams need context

Wrapping images or charts inside a <figure> tag paired with a <figcaption> tells screen readers and search engines that the caption actually belongs to that visual. It also gives you a clean container to style borders, shadows, and spacing all at once.
