Why Simple, Fast Web Pages Feel Better to Read

Notes from learning how line length, whitespace, and a good font scale make long articles way easier on the eyes.

By Alex M. • March 28, 2020 • 6 min read

Everyone has clicked a link to read a quick recipe or tutorial, only to get hit by three popups, an auto-playing video in the corner, and text squished into a tiny strip in the middle. When I started learning HTML and CSS this year, I realized how rare a calm, readable page actually is.

Good editorial design is mostly about getting out of the reader's way. You do not need ten JavaScript libraries to make an article feel like a real magazine. Semantic HTML tags like <article>, <figure>, and <blockquote> already give the page a solid skeleton before you write a single line of CSS.
